# The first piece

Make the user's first pillar piece, then cut it into the first week of posts. The same flow is used every week after, so teach it properly once.

**Before starting:** read `01-founder-identity.md`, `02-content-gps.md`, `03-333-grid.md`, `04-tone-of-voice.md`, `05-content-waterfall.md`, `06-content-bank.csv`.

---

## Part A – Pick the platform and the idea

### Step A.1 – Confirm the engine

Check the engine platform in `02-content-gps.md`.

- If it's **YouTube** → Part B1.
- If it's a **newsletter** → Part B2.
- If it's something else, recommend YouTube or a newsletter for the pillar piece in one or two sentences (YouTube compounds through search; a newsletter is owned and easiest to start). If they still prefer their choice, use the closest flow: B2 for any written long-form (LinkedIn article, carousel, long post), B1 for any video.

### Step A.2 – Pick the idea

1. Open the content bank. Suggest **row 1** or the row from the least-used grid cell.
2. Offer 3 options with one line each on why it's a good first piece. Make the first one the easiest to make well.
3. Let them pick. Mark the row `In progress`.

Good first pieces are ones where they already know the answer cold and have a real example ready.

---

## Part B1 – YouTube video

### Step 1 – Ideation check

Confirm the idea passes three tests. If not, sharpen it before moving on:
- Does it come from one cell of the grid?
- Is there a real thing on screen (a case, a document, a website, numbers)?
- Would their buyer search for it?

### Step 2 – TTH: Title, Thumbnail, Hook

Give **3 different packages**. Each has:
- **Title** – written for search by their ICP. Use their search phrases from `02`. Under ~60 characters. No clickbait they can't deliver.
- **Thumbnail** – a short description: the main visual, 0–4 words of text, the emotion or contrast. It should add to the title, not repeat it.
- **Hook** – the first 30 seconds, written in their voice. State what's at stake for the viewer. No intro, no "hey guys, welcome back", no channel branding.

Let them pick one or mix parts. Refine until they're happy.

### Step 3 – The outline

Write a **bullet outline, not a script.** Talking from bullets sounds natural; reading a script doesn't. Structure:

1. **Hook (0–30s)** – the approved hook.
2. **What we're looking at** – name the thing, one line on why it's worth studying.
3. **3–5 points** – each one: *what it is → why it works → what it costs you not to do it.* Add screen-share cues in brackets, e.g. `[show pricing page]`.
4. **What to change this week** – one concrete action for the viewer.
5. **CTA** – lead magnet first, next step second (from `02`).

Keep bullets short. Three to six words where possible.

### Step 4 – The recording sheet

Give the final sheet in one block they can keep open while recording:
- Title + thumbnail
- The hook (written out)
- The bullet outline with screen cues

Production advice to include once:
- One take. Mistakes are fine, keep going.
- Screen share or simple camera. Light edit: cut the long pauses, nothing more.
- Target 10–20 minutes, but let the content decide.

### Step 5 – After recording

When they're back (or paste the transcript):
- Write the **description**: 2–3 lines on what the video covers, the lead magnet link, the next-step link, then chapters.
- Write a **pinned comment** with the lead magnet.
- Suggest **chapters** from the transcript timestamps if available.
- Give 2 backup titles in case the first one underperforms after a week.

Then go to Part C.

## Part B2 – Newsletter

### Step 1 – Ideation check

Same three tests as YouTube, adapted: grid cell, a real example, a subject line their reader would open.

### Step 2 – Angle and subject line

Give **3 angle options**. Each has:
- **Subject line** – short, specific, curiosity or a clear payoff. No clickbait.
- **Preview text** – the line that shows under the subject.
- **Opening line** – in their voice. Gets to the point immediately.

Let them pick.

### Step 3 – The outline

Default structure (adapt to their newsletter template in `05`):
1. **One thing I saw** – the real example.
2. **One thing I learned** – the insight, with why it matters for the reader.
3. **What to do with it** – one concrete action.
4. **One question** – answerable in one sentence, about the reader's own experience.
5. **Links** – the pillar link if there is one, then the next step.

Get a yes on the outline before drafting.

### Step 4 – Draft

Write the full issue in their voice, following `04-tone-of-voice.md` and their newsletter formatting rules. Then **self-audit** against the tone checklist and fix issues before showing it. Show the draft inline so they can read it.

### Step 5 – Final

Deliver the final issue with subject line and preview text. Offer a file format that pastes well into their newsletter tool if needed. Remind them: reply personally to every answer to the question.

Then go to Part C.

## Part C – Cut it into the first week

This is the waterfall. Use `05-content-waterfall.md` for the cut list and templates, and `references/platforms/` for platform rules.

### Step C.1 – Pull the material

From the outline, transcript or draft, list:
- The strongest single observation.
- Every standalone point.
- Any number, before/after or story.
- The best 60–90 seconds (if video).

### Step C.2 – Hooks first

For each platform in the cut list, give 3–4 hook options. Let them choose before writing full posts.

### Step C.3 – Draft the cuts

Write each piece in their voice, using the platform template. Rules:
- Never paste the same text to two platforms. Rewrite per platform register.
- Links go where `02` says (often first comment or reply, not the post body).
- One CTA per piece, the one from `01`.
- Self-audit each draft against the tone checklist.

Show them in batches by platform.

### Step C.4 – Schedule

If Zernio is connected, follow the scheduling flow in `references/zernio.md`. If not, give them the pieces in posting order with suggested days and times, and offer to set up Zernio now (`references/routines.md` covers when).

### Step C.5 – Log it

Update the content bank row: Status `Live`, Date, Platforms. Leave Result empty for the monthly review. Update `00-progress.md`: first piece done.

### Step C.6 – Next

Say: "That's your first week. Next we set up your routine so this happens every week without thinking about it." Go to `references/routines.md`.
